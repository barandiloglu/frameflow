Mark · İşaret · II

A ligature for a network.

Bir ağ için, tek bir bağ.

Before the films, the flag. The mark we made for CTBDH is a lower-case ct ligature: the C’s arc and the T’s stem share a single curve, the way two business cards share a coffee table. One letter for Canada, one for Türkiye — and a slash through both, dropping from upper right to lower left like a signature underline.

It lives in two registers. Red on black is the network voice — the umbrella, the masthead, the credit card. Mark alone on paper is the field voice — for member businesses to flag affiliation on their own surfaces without adopting CTBDH’s house styling wholesale.

No. 01 · The ligature

Two letters, one curve.

The C and the T share a single bowl — the C’s arc continues directly into the T’s crossbar without breaking. It’s the whole brief in one shape: two countries, one handshake.

No. 02 · The slash

A line across both.

A diagonal stroke cuts from the T’s shoulder down through the bowl of the C — half punctuation, half passport stamp. It binds the two letters into a single glyph so the mark reads as a unit, not a pair.

No. 03 · The wordmark

Bilingual by default.

Canada · Türkiye · Business · Development · Hub. Five words, five lines, Inter 500. Türkiye keeps its diacritic; the wordmark is never anglicised. The stack falls to the right of the mark in the primary lockup.
