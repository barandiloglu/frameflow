Letter from the Director · pp. 02–03

A note from the cockpit.

Most of our prospective clients arrive through Turkish-language search and word-of-mouth. They are looking for two things at once: warmth and authority. A regulated immigration consultancy must be both, and a programme like this one has to feel that way too. In this catalogue you will find five films — one for each of our practice areas — presented as the work it actually is, with the standard a Regulated Canadian Immigration Consultant is required to bring to it.

FrameFlow Studio joined the firm in spring 2025. The brief was short. Findable. Bookable. Trustworthy. In two languages. The work that followed is the schedule you see here: a marketing site at northernpathways.ca with twelve routes per locale; a regulator-grade CRS calculator at the box office; a Six Selection Factors eligibility checker with a 67/100 cutoff; a live IRCC draws tracker indexing every Express Entry & PNP draw since 2015; and a Turkish-language Instagram, @kanadadayeniyasam, that mirrors every draw to the diaspora the morning it lands.

Each film carries a real synopsis, a runtime derived from IRCC processing times, and a rating that signals procedural complexity. The cast is the underlying programmes — FSWP, CEC, PNP, spousal sponsorship, the PGWP, and so on. The director, in every case, is the captain who signs the manifest.

If our work this year has a thesis, it is this: treat clients as individuals, not case numbers. The five films that follow are an attempt to do that on screen.

B. Akyol

FILM № 01

Express Entry.

A federal selection drama in five acts — biography plotted against IRCC’s grid.

An applicant’s biography — age, education, language proficiency, work history, adaptability — is plotted against IRCC’s Comprehensive Ranking System. The lead character receives an Invitation to Apply at one of the federal draw cutoffs (currently a moving target between 421 and 736 depending on category) or doesn’t. Counsel works to maximise the score across all four sections (Core, Spouse, Skill Transferability, Additional) and to time the application against the next favourable category-based draw — Healthcare, French, Trades, PNP. Includes the Federal Skilled Worker, Canadian Experience Class, and Provincial Nominee streams as supporting features.

FILM № 02

Family Connect.

A domestic drama about reuniting families across borders — sponsorship as the central act.

The lead character — a Canadian citizen or permanent resident — sponsors a partner, child, or parent. Counsel prepares the sponsorship undertaking (a 3–20-year financial commitment depending on relationship), the application package, and the response to procedural fairness if it lands. The parents and grandparents stream runs on a 36-month lottery cycle. Spousal sponsorship requires the bona fides of the relationship to be demonstrated through joint accounts, leases, photographs — the audit is humane but it is an audit. Counsel translates the relationship into the documentation IRCC will accept.

FILM № 03

Temporary Residence.

A short-form anthology of three storylines that share a common arc — dual intent, ties home, means of support.

Three storylines — Visitor, Study, Work — that share a common arc: the applicant proves dual intent, sufficient ties to the home country, and means of support for the duration of the stay. The visa officer is the antagonist of every act, and the antagonist is fair if you give the antagonist what is required. Counsel selects the right permit class (the Post-Graduation Work Permit, the LMIA-exempt work permit under one of the 30+ exemption categories, or a study permit at a Designated Learning Institution), prepares the application, and anticipates the reasons for refusal — insufficient ties, inadequate funds, intent to remain.

FILM № 04

The PR Card.

A documentary about residency obligations — 730 days inside, in any rolling 1,825.

Permanent residents must spend 730 days inside Canada in any rolling 5-year period to maintain status. Counsel prepares the renewal, defends the residency calculation against an underage with humanitarian considerations or a pending appeal at the IRB, or applies for a Permanent Resident Travel Document for clients abroad whose card has expired. The document is necessary to board a Canada-bound flight. Counsel calculates days, prepares evidence (passport stamps, employer letters, travel records), and represents at the IAD if removal is initiated.

The final act · the long arc.

FILM № 05

Citizenship.

The trilogy capstone — 1,095 days, a knowledge test, and the Oath.

After 1,095 days of physical presence in the previous five years, a permanent resident applies for Canadian citizenship. The lead studies for the knowledge test (a written or oral assessment of Canadian rights, responsibilities, history, geography, and government), sits the assessment, and takes the Oath of Citizenship at a ceremony presided over by a citizenship judge. Counsel prepares the application, attends the test if required, and represents at the ceremony. The runtime in IRCC’s current backlog is approximately 12–18 months from filing to oath; resumption and retention applications follow a similar arc.

— Director profile · the captain

Burcu Akyol.

Director of all five films in this season’s programme; principal of Northern Pathways Immigration Consulting; Regulated Canadian Immigration Consultant (RCIC-IRB) in good standing with the College of Immigration and Citizenship Consultants. Adjunct professor at Queen’s University teaching the regulatory framework she practises within. Board Member of the Canadian Association of Professional Immigration Consultants.

Akyol opened the practice in 2019 with a thesis: that a regulated immigration consultancy can be both warm and authoritative if — and only if — the regulator’s standard becomes the floor, not the ceiling. The practice grew through Turkish-language word-of-mouth in the diaspora; today most of its prospects arrive through search in their first language, and find a counsel who speaks both.

She represents at the Immigration Appeals Division, the Refugee Protection Division, and on procedural fairness at all levels. She does not file what counsel cannot stand behind — doctrine 01 of the operating manual, made operational on every page of the website.

Buy your ticket at the box office.

The score on screen tells you whether your ticket admits to film 01, 02, 03, or whether you sit in the lobby for a season. Each session ends on a signed PDF stub that the captain (counsel of record) tears at admission — the same PDF the client takes to a consult.
