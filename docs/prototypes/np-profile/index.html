Subject · RCIC-IRB · CICC member An immigration practice told in two languages. Photographed in Toronto

Profile By the editors of T·Magazine

The captain at her desk — and the website, the calculator, the social handle that brings the diaspora to her door.

It begins, as these things often do, with a phone call. Burcu Akyol is at her desk in North York when the file lands — a software engineer in Istanbul, two children, a Master’s degree, and an English exam yet to be sat. She does not pick up a brochure. She opens a tab. On the tab is a Comprehensive Ranking System calculator she helped specify; a website she signed off on in two languages; and somewhere in a tab adjacent, a Turkish-language Instagram handle that the same engineer's cousin probably scrolled three nights ago.

This is the practice, and this is its season. Akyol is a Regulated Canadian Immigration Consultant in good standing with the College of Immigration and Citizenship Consultants; an adjunct professor at Queen’s University; a CAPIC Board Member. The practice she leads — Northern Pathways Immigration Consulting — engaged FrameFlow Studio in Toronto in the spring of 2025. The brief was short and the standard, less so.

“Findable. Bookable. Trustworthy. In two languages.” That is what Akyol asked for. What she meant by it took the next year to build.

Most of the firm’s prospects arrive through Turkish- language search and word-of-mouth in the diaspora. This means a regulated practice has to do a thing that regulated practices are not always good at doing: speak the language people search in, and speak it without shedding the authority a regulator requires. The website at northernpathways.ca serves twelve routes per locale at /en and /tr, with first-class language detection. The home view leads with Akyol’s name and the seal of her credentials. She is, in this layout and in life, the face of the firm before the form is.

The calculator at the centre of the site is the practice made portable. It honours IRCC's 2026 grid — including the removal of arranged-employment points mid-year — and exits each session on a one-click PDF Akyol can sign as counsel of record. The companion eligibility checker for the Federal Skilled Worker programme is a binary gate at 67/100. Both run in two languages, with proper tooltips on every input. Akyol, asked what she wanted from these tools, has said the same thing twice: “I want them to be honest.”

Treat clients as individuals, not case numbers. Tell them what the College’s standard is. Tell them what a draw cutoff is. Tell them the price up front. Then ask them what they want. From the brand voice · Northern Pathways · 2026

Twenty paces from her office sits the part of the practice that does not file applications, that has no docket and carries no licence number: the firm’s Turkish-language Instagram, @kanadadayeniyasam — “new life in Canada.” 248 posts shipped this season. Draw-day cards mirrored to the feed within minutes of IRCC publishing. Reels recorded between consults, often in the cockpit of two cameras Akyol keeps on her desk. Plain-language explainers in EN and TR, captioned for both registers, never for one or the other.

The diaspora arrives in two languages.
We meet them in both.

— Burcu Akyol, on what care looks like in immigration consulting.

This is the part of the year where the prose risks listing deliverables, and the editors of this magazine resist that risk only with difficulty. So consider, briefly: a marketing site at northernpathways.ca with twelve routes per locale; a regulator-grade CRS calculator; a Six Selection Factors eligibility checker; a live IRCC draws tracker indexing every Express Entry & PNP draw since 2015; SEO in two locales; and the social programme above. Each one was specified, built, reviewed, and signed by Akyol against a list of five values she made FrameFlow tape to a wall: ethical guidance, personalised service, transparency in pricing, knowledge and expertise, care and lived experience.

If a profile of an immigration counsel seems an unlikely subject for the spring magazine, consider what spring is, in this category. It is the season when the levels plan lands; when the Working for Workers Seven Act is read for the third time; when Ontario's new programs come online; when the IRCC adjusts a grid that 3,000 prospective Canadians are within four points of. Akyol has watched all of these things this spring and posted on each. Each post links back to the right calculator in the right locale.

It is now late afternoon. The phone has rung twice; one of the calls was a regulator's office in Ottawa, the other a worried mother in Mersin. Akyol takes both, in the right language, with the right register. The website on the screen behind her closes a session with the words “Designed and developed by Frame Flow,” a small line in the footer of every one of its twelve routes. The captain's licence is on the wall above the desk. The next webinar is scheduled for the eleventh of November. It will run in Turkish and English. It will not run over.

Probably it will run over. Akyol’s consults often do.
